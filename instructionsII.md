0. DOCUMENT STATUS AND SCOPE
======================================================================

This document describes an experimental process for producing a cast,
pressed, and dried web from defatted hemp seed protein powder, water, and
optional non-iodized sodium chloride.

Product designation:

"Experimental hemp seed protein powder paper roll, untested."

Short status label:

"PAPER ROLL, UNTESTED."

This is a pilot-development protocol. It is not a validated commercial
papermaking specification.

Hemp seed protein powder contains particles rather than the long cellulose
fibers that form conventional paper. The process therefore produces a
protein-based cast web rather than ordinary cellulose paper.

The material must not be represented as office paper, copy paper, printing
paper, drawing paper, archival paper, packaging paper, recyclable paper,
compostable paper, biodegradable paper, or another recognized paper grade
unless appropriate testing establishes those properties.

======================================================================
1. INGREDIENTS
======================================================================

Required:

1. Thoroughly defatted hemp seed protein powder
2. Water

Optional:

3. Non-iodized sodium chloride

Do not use:

- road salt;
- curing salt;
- salt containing nitrite or nitrate;
- salt containing unverified additives;
- solvents;
- alcohol;
- fuels;
- oxidizers;
- metal powders;
- ignition-promoting additives;
- oils, waxes, or synthetic release agents if the formulation must remain
  limited to powder, water, and salt.

Salt is an experimental process variable. It is not the primary binder.

Controlled hydration and heat activation of hemp protein provide the
principal binding mechanism.

======================================================================
3. IMPORTANT TECHNICAL LIMITATIONS
======================================================================

Hemp seed protein powder does not contain a long-fiber network.

The web may consequently have:

- low wet strength;
- limited tensile strength;
- low tear resistance;
- limited folding endurance;
- sensitivity to liquid water;
- sensitivity to humidity;
- dimensional instability;
- drying shrinkage;
- edge cracking;
- blocking during winding;
- limited unsupported-web strength;
- difficulty releasing from a carrier;
- inconsistent surface cohesion;
- short storage life if retained moisture permits microbial activity.

The first roll trials must remain supported on a fine carrier fabric or
release belt through forming, pressing, and most of the drying process.

Do not attempt a free-standing wet web until supported trials demonstrate
adequate continuity and handling strength.

9. POWDER PREPARATION
======================================================================

1. Begin with hemp seed press cake remaining after oil extraction.

2. Use thoroughly defatted material.

3. Dry below 50°C / 122°F.

4. Target approximately 6–10% powder moisture.

5. Do not scorch, brown, or burn the material.

6. Mill the dried press cake.

7. Separate it into approximate particle-size fractions.

Fine fraction:

50–100 micrometres

Structural fraction:

100–250 micrometres

8. Use fine powder in Modules A, B, and E.

9. Use structural powder in Module C.

10. Remove hull fragments, stones, metal, and foreign material.

Reject powder that:

- contains visible mold;
- smells rancid;
- smells burned;
- releases visible free oil when compressed;
- contains hard moisture-damaged lumps;
- contains foreign material;
- cannot be dispersed in water.

Excess residual oil may cause:

- translucent spots;
- greasy surfaces;
- poor cohesion;
- uneven drying;
- carrier contamination;
- weak surface coating;
- blocking during winding.

======================================================================
10. MODULE A: UNHEATED BINDER PULP
======================================================================

Purpose:

Create a hydrated, unheated protein phase intended to reduce the brittleness
of the heat-activated protein phase.

Procedure:

1. Charge Module A water into a clean mixer.

2. Begin moderate-to-high agitation.

3. Add the fine Module A powder gradually.

4. Do not dump the powder into one location.

5. Mix for 10 minutes.

6. Hydrate for 30 minutes.

7. Remix for 5 minutes.

8. Do not heat Module A.

Target condition:

- smooth;
- pourable;
- no dry pockets;
- no large lumps;
- no continuous oil layer;
- able to circulate through the mixer.

======================================================================
11. MODULE B: HEAT-ACTIVATED PROTEIN GEL
======================================================================

Purpose:

Create the principal binding phase through controlled protein hydration and
heat activation.

Procedure:

1. Charge Module B water into a temperature-controlled vessel.

2. Begin agitation.

3. Add Module B fine powder gradually.

4. Mix for 10 minutes.

5. Hydrate for 30 minutes.

6. Heat gradually to 72–76°C / 162–169°F.

7. Hold for 7–10 minutes.

8. Mix continuously during heating.

9. Do not boil.

10. Cool to 50–55°C / 122–131°F.

Target condition:

- thicker than Module A;
- smooth rather than rubbery;
- capable of continuous flow;
- no hard gel particles;
- no burned material;
- no free oil layer.

If rubber-like pieces form:

- reduce the hold to 5–7 minutes;
- lower the activation temperature slightly;
- heat more gradually;
- improve agitation;
- record any added adjustment water.

======================================================================
12. MODULE C: STRUCTURAL PROTEIN PASTE
======================================================================

Purpose:

Create body and dimensional structure without converting all the powder into
a continuous smooth film.

Procedure:

1. Charge Module C water into a separate vessel.

2. Add Module C structural powder gradually.

3. Mix until every particle is wetted.

4. Hydrate for 15–20 minutes.

5. Do not heat.

6. Do not grind the paste completely smooth.

Target condition:

A thick, slightly granular, spreadable paste.

Too much coarse material may cause:

- flaking;
- poor internal cohesion;
- holes;
- uneven thickness.

Too little coarse material may produce:

- excessive shrinkage;
- overly smooth film;
- high density;
- poor dimensional stability.

======================================================================
13. MODULE D: OPTIONAL SALT STREAM
======================================================================

Purpose:

Test whether a small salt dose changes protein dispersion or gel structure.

Procedure:

1. Warm Module D water to 35–45°C / 95–113°F.

2. Add non-iodized sodium chloride.

3. Mix until dissolved.

4. Confirm that no visible crystals remain.

5. Do not add dry salt directly to concentrated protein pulp.

6. Prepare an otherwise identical salt-free control using plain water.

Remove salt from the formulation if it produces:

- white surface crystals;
- greater humidity sensitivity;
- increased water absorption;
- poorer bending;
- tackiness;
- corrosion;
- reduced cohesion;
- inconsistent surfaces;
- blocking after winding.

======================================================================
14. MODULE E: FINE SURFACE PULP
======================================================================

Purpose:

Produce a matte, cohesive, non-flaking surface on both sides of the roll.

Procedure:

1. Charge Module E water into a clean vessel.

2. Add the finest powder gradually.

3. Mix for 10 minutes.

4. Hydrate for 20 minutes.

5. Remix.

6. Screen the mixture if visible grit remains.

Target condition:

- smooth;
- thin;
- coatable;
- free of coarse particles;
- free of lumps;
- no separated oil.

Reserve Module E until the core web has been partially dried.

======================================================================
15. FIRST RECOMBINATION: BINDER MATRIX
======================================================================

1. Keep Module A under moderate agitation.

2. Meter warm Module B into Module A over 5–10 minutes.

3. Do not add Module B to a stationary vessel.

4. Mix for another 5 minutes.

5. Avoid excessive foam and entrained air.

Result:

Hydrated unheated protein
+
Heat-activated protein gel
=
Continuous protein binder matrix

The mixture should remain homogeneous and free of hard or rubber-like lumps.

======================================================================
16. SECOND RECOMBINATION: SALT CONDITIONING
======================================================================

Salt trial:

1. Keep the binder matrix moving.

2. Meter Module D into it over 3–5 minutes.

3. Mix for 5 minutes.

4. Inspect for coagulation, clumping, or separation.

Salt-free control:

1. Add the same quantity of plain water.

2. Use the same addition time.

3. Use the same mixing speed and duration.

If clumps appear:

- stop adding salt solution;
- record the amount already added;
- do not add more salt;
- repeat the next trial with half the salt or no salt.

======================================================================
17. THIRD RECOMBINATION: STRUCTURAL PULP
======================================================================

1. Reduce mixing speed.

2. Add Module C in small portions over 5–10 minutes.

3. Mix gently after every addition.

4. Continue mixing for 5 minutes after the final addition.

5. Preserve controlled particle structure.

Target furnish:

- cohesive;
- pumpable at low shear;
- spreadable;
- free of dry powder;
- free of hard gel lumps;
- free of visible salt;
- free of separated oil;
- capable of forming a continuous layer on the carrier.

21. CARRIER-SUPPORTED ROLL FORMING
======================================================================

Use a fine polyester carrier fabric or compatible release belt.

1. Clean the carrier.

2. Tension it evenly.

3. Set wet-web width to approximately 9.0–9.5 inches.

4. Feed the furnish to a coating or casting head.

5. Deposit a uniform layer on the moving carrier.

6. Use a doctor blade, adjustable gate, slot, or coating bar to control wet
   thickness.

7. Begin at approximately 0.1–0.5 m/min.

8. Use the lowest speed needed for uniform deposition.

9. Prevent pulsating feed.

10. Keep the feed vessel under slow agitation.

11. Avoid high shear after final recombination.

12. Measure wet width and thickness every 1–2 metres.

13. Do not repeatedly recycle heat-treated protein.

======================================================================
22. WET-DEPOSITION CONTROL
======================================================================

At 150 g/m² and a width of 0.2159 m:

Dry material required:

32.39 g per linear metre

At 0.5 m/min:

32.39 × 0.5 = approximately 16.2 g dry solids per minute

If measured furnish solids are 16.7%:

16.2 ÷ 0.167 = approximately 97 g wet furnish per minute

Always recalculate feed using measured furnish solids.

Powder moisture, evaporation, adjustment water, and vessel residue can alter
the actual solids concentration.

======================================================================
23. INITIAL DEWATERING
======================================================================

1. Allow the deposited web to settle for 1–3 minutes.

2. Use absorbent contact, gentle vacuum, or controlled warm airflow.

3. Do not apply strong vacuum.

4. Keep the layer supported.

5. Remove visible free water before pressing.

6. Inspect the edges for sideways flow.

7. Reduce deposited thickness or pressure if material moves beyond the target
   width.

======================================================================

======================================================================

Use carrier-supported nips or flat pilot pressing.

First press:

0.10–0.20 MPa
15–29 psi

Stopped-pilot dwell:

1–2 minutes

Second press:

0.30–0.50 MPa
44–73 psi

Stopped-pilot dwell:

2–3 minutes

Final wet press:

Approximately 0.75 MPa

- reduce surface texture;
- produce gloss;
- create thickness variation;
- trap water;
- cause sticking;
- tear the web.

======================================================================
25. PARTIAL ROLL DRYING
======================================================================

Keep the web on the carrier.

Initial drying:

35–40°C
95–104°F

Purpose:

Remove free water without forming a hard surface skin.

Second drying:

40–45°C
104–113°F

Purpose:

Produce a firm but still slightly damp web.

Use balanced airflow where possible.

Do not dry completely before applying Module E.

======================================================================
26. CONTINUOUS SURFACE COATING
======================================================================

1. Remix Module E at low speed.

2. Apply a thin, uniform coat to the primary surface.

3. Use a controlled blade, slot, coating bar, or suitable roll applicator.

4. Do not flood the web.

5. Allow the coating to set.

6. Apply a lighter balancing coat to the reverse surface where equipment
   permits.

Recommended distribution:

Primary surface:

Approximately 60% of Module E

Reverse surface:

Approximately 40% of Module E

If both surfaces cannot be coated continuously:

1. Coat the primary surface.
2. Partially dry.
3. Wind at very low tension.
4. Condition sufficiently to prevent blocking.
5. Unwind and reverse the roll.
6. Apply the reverse coating.

======================================================================
27. FINAL LIGHT PRESSING
======================================================================

After the coating has set:

Pressure:

0.30–0.50 MPa
44–73 psi

The objective is to anchor the coating, not polish the web.

Excessive pressure may:

- reduce surface texture;
- create gloss;
- increase density;
- reduce flexibility;
- force coating material sideways;
- increase bending cracks.

======================================================================
29. CARRIER RELEASE
======================================================================

1. Cool the web near room temperature.

2. Separate it at a shallow angle.

3. Support the released web immediately.

4. Do not pull sharply.

5. Stop if it stretches, tears, or delaminates.

If sticking occurs:

- reduce Module B activation severity;
- confirm adequate drying;
- cool completely;
- reduce surface-coat thickness;
- reduce final press pressure;
- evaluate a more suitable reusable carrier.

======================================================================
30. WINDING
======================================================================

Recommended pilot core diameter:

At least 150 mm
Approximately 6 in

1. Wind at minimum stable tension.

2. Do not wind tightly.

3. Do not wind a warm or damp web.

4. Use an inert temporary interleaf if blocking occurs.

5. Stop if the web cracks around the core.

Record:

- winding tension;
- core diameter;
- roll diameter;
- web temperature;
- estimated moisture;
- cracking;
- blocking;
- telescoping;
- edge damage.

======================================================================
31. ROLL CONDITIONING
======================================================================

Recommended conditions:

Temperature:

Approximately 23°C / 73°F

Relative humidity:

Approximately 50%

Time:

24–48 hours

Keep away from:

- liquid water;
- condensation;
- high humidity;
- heaters;
- direct sunlight;
- flames;
- sparks;
- ignition sources.

Do not seal a warm or damp roll in plastic.

======================================================================
32. CUTTING INTO 8.5 × 11-INCH SHEETS
======================================================================

1. Unwind at low tension.

2. Trim web width to 8.5 inches / 215.9 mm.

3. Cross-cut every 11 inches / 279.4 mm.

4. Keep the primary surface orientation consistent.

5. Reject sections containing:

   - holes;
   - hard gel;
   - oily spots;
   - salt crystals;
   - severe curl;
   - cracks;
   - loose powder;
   - thickness bands;
   - coating streaks;
   - delamination;
   - sticky areas.

6. Stack converted sheets under light, flat restraint for 24 hours.

======================================================================
33. FINISHED-SURFACE TARGETS
======================================================================

Desired qualities:

- natural tan or brown appearance;
- matte finish;
- uniform fine texture;
- no loose powder;
- no visible salt crystals;
- no oily areas;
- no tackiness;
- no hard gel particles;
- limited curl;
- no surface delamination;
- adequate cohesion for ordinary handling;
- ability to bend around a large core without immediate cracking.

Do not polish or calender the first trials.

Suitability for marking, printing, coating, packaging, or another application
must be evaluated separately.

======================================================================
34. BASIC SURFACE-MARKING TEST
======================================================================

Test with:

- HB graphite pencil;
- 2B graphite pencil;
- ballpoint pen;
- wax crayon.

Procedure:

1. Make light-pressure lines.
2. Make medium-pressure lines.
3. Make firm-pressure lines.
4. Inspect under consistent lighting.
5. Rub part of the marked area with clean paper.
6. Attempt light erasing where applicable.

Record:

- line visibility;
- scratching;
- flaking;
- indentation;
- smearing;
- surface lifting;
- loose-particle production;
- cracking.

This test does not establish suitability as office or drawing paper.

======================================================================
35. DRY-PARTICLE SURFACE TEST
======================================================================

Test with a dry material such as charcoal or chalk.

1. Make light lines.
2. Make firm-pressure lines.
3. Create a shaded area.
4. Rub part of the area.
5. Hold the sheet vertically.
6. Tap it gently.
7. Inspect for surface damage.

Record:

- particle retention;
- abrasion;
- powder loss;
- flaking;
- cracking;
- lifting;
- delamination.

This is a surface-cohesion test, not a product-grade classification.

======================================================================
36. PHYSICAL HANDLING TESTS
======================================================================

Test the following:

1. Bend around a 150 mm core.
2. Unwind after 24 hours.
3. Make one gentle 180-degree fold.
4. Stack sheets under light pressure.
5. Condition at controlled humidity.

8. Measure dimensional change.

39. TROUBLESHOOTING
======================================================================

PROBLEM:
Web tears before drying.

LIKELY CAUSE:
No long-fiber wet-strength network.

CORRECTION:
Keep the web supported longer, reduce speed, extend initial drying, reduce
first-press pressure, or reduce wet thickness.

--------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
Web cracks during winding.

LIKELY CAUSE:
Core too small, excessive dryness, excessive web thickness, high tension, or
excessive Module B activation.

CORRECTION:
Use a larger core, lower tension, reduce basis weight, shorten Module B
activation, or reduce final drying severity.

--------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
Web sticks to itself.

LIKELY CAUSE:
Web wound warm, damp, or tacky.

CORRECTION:
Cool completely, extend drying, reduce surface-coat thickness, reduce winding
tension, or use a temporary inert interleaf.

--------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
Surface flakes during handling.

LIKELY CAUSE:
Insufficient surface binder or excessive structural powder.

CORRECTION:
Increase Module E from 5% to 7.5% and reduce Module C by the same amount.

--------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
Surface is excessively smooth.

LIKELY CAUSE:
Excessive pressure or insufficient structural texture.

CORRECTION:
Reduce final pressure and preserve controlled particle texture.

--------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
Sheet cracks when bent.

LIKELY CAUSE:
Sheet too thick, too dry, or over-gelled.

CORRECTION:
Reduce basis weight, shorten heat activation, reduce final temperature, use a
larger core, or reduce winding tension.

--------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
White crystals appear.

LIKELY CAUSE:
Salt migration.

CORRECTION:
Eliminate salt.

--------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
Sheet softens in humidity.

LIKELY CAUSE:
Moisture absorption by protein and salt.

CORRECTION:
Eliminate salt first and compare specimens only after equal conditioning.

--------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
Oily spots appear.

LIKELY CAUSE:
Excess residual oil.

CORRECTION:
Use more thoroughly defatted powder.

--------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
Thickness bands appear.

LIKELY CAUSE:
Pulsating feed, uneven gate, unstable viscosity, or uneven carrier tension.

CORRECTION:
Stabilize flow, level the casting head, control temperature, maintain slow
agitation, and inspect belt tension.

--------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
Hard gel particles appear.

LIKELY CAUSE:
Module B heated too rapidly or too long.

CORRECTION:
Heat more slowly, increase agitation, shorten activation, or screen Module B
before recombination.

40. SAFETY AND PROCESS CONTROLS
======================================================================

Fine hemp seed protein powder is an organic dust.

Required precautions:

- control airborne powder;
- eliminate flames and sparks;
- prevent buildup near motors and heaters;
- use appropriate eye and respiratory protection;
- guard mixers, rolls, presses, cutters, and winders;
- use lockout procedures before cleaning machinery;
- do not leave heated mixtures unattended;
- treat dry trim and dust as combustible organic material;
- verify dryer ventilation;
- evaluate salt-water corrosion;
- evaluate wastewater solids and biological load;
- validate safe press loading;
- obtain engineering review before scale-up.

Do not add substances intended to make the web ignite or burn more readily.

Determine ignition, flame spread, smoke, and heat release through controlled
laboratory testing before making any claim about fire performance.
